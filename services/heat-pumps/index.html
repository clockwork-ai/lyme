Heat pumps that aren't doing their job

A lot of heat pumps get fitted and left on the wrong settings, or put on radiators and pipes that were never sized for one. The house doesn't get warm, or it does and the bills are too high. I'm not selling new heat pumps. I put right the ones that are already in.

What I get called for

  • Flow temperature set too high, so it costs more to run than it should
  • Weather compensation not set up, or switched off
  • Short cycling, where it switches on and off all day
  • Rooms that never get warm enough
  • Radiators too small for the lower temperatures a heat pump runs at
  • Pipework too small to carry the flow the heat pump needs
  • Buffer tanks, low loss headers and valves fighting the heat pump
  • Old thermostats and programmers set up as if it were a boiler
  • Hot water settings, including the cylinder's weekly hot cycle
  • Air and sludge in the system, and blocked filters

What happens when I turn up

  1. Tell me the make, roughly how old it is, and what's wrong: cold rooms, high bills, noise, or switching on and off. Photos of the unit, the controls and the cylinder help.
  2. I look at the whole system, not just the box outside: the settings, the pipework, the radiators and the controls.
  3. I tell you what's wrong and what it'll take to put right. You get a price before I do the work.
  4. I work on the water side. If the fault is in the refrigerant circuit inside the unit, I'll tell you straight and point you to someone qualified for that.

Questions

Why does my heat pump cost so much to run?

Often it's been set up like a boiler: a high flow temperature, no weather compensation, and controls that keep switching it on and off. Getting those right is usually the first job.

Can you sort out a heat pump someone else fitted?

Yes. That's most of my heat pump work.

Do you fit new heat pumps?

No. I'm not selling heat pumps. I put right the ones that are already in.

Do you work on the refrigerant side?

No. I work on the water side: settings, pipework, radiators, pumps and controls. If it's the refrigerant circuit, I'll tell you and point you to someone qualified.

It's still under the installer's warranty. Should I call you?

Check your warranty first. If the installer still covers it, ask them to come back. If they won't, or can't fix it, ring me.
